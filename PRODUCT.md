# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Small creative teams: designers, writers, indie studios, and early-stage startup squads of 2–15 people. They're using Jackal during active project work — brainstorming, task tracking, async check-ins, and live collaboration sessions. They're comfortable with modern tools (Figma, Linear, Slack) and have zero patience for setup friction or corporate-flavored UI.

## Product Purpose

Jackal is a real-time collaborative workspace built around visual boards. Teams create posts (notes, questions, task lists), attach images, reply in threads, and arrange content on a dot-grid canvas. It exists because existing tools are either too heavy (Notion, Jira) or too limited (Trello) for creative teams who want the speed of a group whiteboard with the structure of a task tracker. Success looks like a team that reaches for Jackal first because it's the fastest, most enjoyable place to think together.

## Brand Personality

Fast, warm, alive. Jackal should feel like Linear's precision crossed with the warmth of something made by people who actually enjoy building things. The palette — frozen-lake blue, tangerine, mint, ivory against deep carbon — is not decorative: it's how Jackal communicates that this tool is different. Energetic without being loud. Playful without being juvenile. Every interaction should feel snappy and considered.

## Anti-references

- **Notion**: Dense hierarchy, document-heavy surfaces, infinite nesting, "just write anything anywhere" formlessness. Jackal has opinions about structure.
- **Generic SaaS landing pages**: Cream/warm-sand backgrounds, bento grids of identical feature cards, "ABOUT / FEATURES / PRICING" eyebrows on every section, hero-metric number grids, streamline/empower/supercharge copy.
- **Jira**: Enterprise grey, configuration-first, intimidating information density, the feeling that the software is tracking you.

## Design Principles

1. **The canvas is alive** — real-time feedback, motion, and color are product features, not decoration. The board should visibly respond to the people on it.
2. **Color earns its keep** — the full palette (frozen-lake, tangerine, mint) is used with confidence and purpose, not sprinkled as accent. Each hue carries meaning across post types and states.
3. **Speed of intent** — every interaction should feel instant. Optimistic UI, snappy transitions, zero loading spinners where avoidable. If it feels laggy, it's broken.
4. **Show the thing doing the thing** — mockups, live previews, and product demos outperform feature lists. The landing page and onboarding show Jackal in motion, not described in bullets.
5. **Warmth over formality** — interfaces are built by people for people. Personality in empty states, micro-copy that sounds human, details that make users smile the third time they notice them.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text minimum 4.5:1 contrast against background; large/bold text minimum 3:1. Full keyboard navigation for all interactive elements. Visible focus indicators. Every animation respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. Color is never the sole carrier of meaning.
